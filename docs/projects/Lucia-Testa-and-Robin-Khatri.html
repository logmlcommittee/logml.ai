Topology-Aware Learning for Spatial Transcriptomics

Author

Lucia Testa, Robin Khatri

Lucia Testa Robin Khatri

Lucia Testa

Lucia Testa is a Postdoctoral Researcher at University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf (UKE), Germany. She earned both her Master’s degree and PhD in Data Science from Sapienza University of Rome, where she completed her PhD in 2025 under the supervision of Prof. Sergio Barbarossa. Her doctoral research introduced new approaches to topological signal processing and learning over uncertain domains.

Her current interests focus on Topological Deep Learning and its applications to Computational Biology and Computational Chemistry. In 2021, she was awarded the Best Master’s Thesis Award by Fondazione Sapienza.

Robin Khatri

Robin Khatri is a postdoctoral researcher at the Institute of Medical Systems Bioinformatics (IMSB) & III Department of Medicine, University Medical Center Hamburg- Eppendorf (UKE), Germany. He earned his PhD in 2025 from the Faculty of Computer Science at the University of Hamburg, working in systems biology under the supervision of Prof. Stefan Bonn. His doctoral research developed deep learning methods for the computational estimation of cell type composition and dynamics in single-cell and spatial omics data. He previously completed an MSc in Computer Science with a university diploma in Machine Learning at Jean Monnet University, Saint-Étienne, France. His current research focuses on machine learning methods for spatial transcriptomics with applications to autoimmune kidney diseases and glioblastoma. He is an Add-on Fellow of the Joachim Herz Foundation for Interdisciplinary Life Sciences (2024–2026) and a fellow of the UKE iPrime Medical Scientist Programme (2026).

Project

Spatial transcriptomics (ST) measures gene expression while preserving spatial context, enabling the study of tissue architecture and local cellular neighborhoods in situ [1]. Many existing computational pipelines for ST rely on graph-based models, which have been very successful for tasks such as spatial domain identification and representation learning, but are naturally limited to pairwise relations between spots or cells [2,3]. This project explores whether topological deep learning can provide a richer framework for ST by explicitly modeling higher-order spatial interactions, such as small multi-spot microenvironments, through simplicial or cell complexes [4,5].

We will represent a tissue section both as a graph and as a higher-order topological object built from spatial adjacency, then compare standard graph baselines with topology-aware models that propagate information over nodes, edges, and higher-order structures [2,4]. The technical goal is to test whether such topological representations can improve tasks such as spatial domain segmentation, denoising or reconstruction, and the identification of biologically meaningful tissue regions [2,3,6]. More broadly, the project will investigate whether higher-order spatial interactions can serve as a useful inductive bias for learning informative representations of tissue architecture while improving downstream tasks.
